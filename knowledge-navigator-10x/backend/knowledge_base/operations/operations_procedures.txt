DEUTSCHE BANK OPERATIONS & SERVICE MANAGEMENT PROCEDURES
DOCUMENT ID: DB-OPS-PROC-2026-V1.4
CLASSIFICATION: INTERNAL USE ONLY

1. INTRODUCTION
This comprehensive document outlines the standard operating procedures, guidelines, and expectations for the Deutsche Bank Operations and Service Management divisions. The primary objective is to ensure consistent, high-quality service delivery across all business units while maintaining stringent adherence to regulatory requirements and internal policies. These procedures are binding for all personnel within the Operations department and related service functions.

2. SERVICE LEVEL AGREEMENTS (SLAs)
The cornerstone of our service delivery model is strict adherence to our defined Service Level Agreements (SLAs). These SLAs represent our commitment to internal stakeholders and external clients regarding the timeliness and reliability of our support functions.
- Priority 1 (P1) Incidents: These are critical issues causing widespread business disruption or significant financial/reputational risk. The mandatory resolution time for P1 incidents is within 4 hours of the initial report. Constant communication is required.
- Priority 2 (P2) Incidents: These are high-impact issues affecting multiple users or a key business process, but where a temporary workaround may exist. The resolution time for P2 incidents is strictly within 24 hours.
- Priority 3 (P3) Incidents: These represent medium to low impact issues, such as single-user localized problems or general service requests. These must be resolved within 5 business days.

3. INCIDENT MANAGEMENT PROCESS
A structured approach to incident management is crucial for minimizing downtime and ensuring a rapid return to normal operations. The standard lifecycle is as follows:
- Identification: Recognizing that an incident has occurred through automated monitoring alerts, user reports, or routine checks.
- Logging: Every incident must be formally recorded in the enterprise IT Service Management tool (ServiceNow) without exception.
- Classification: Categorizing the incident based on its impact, urgency, and affected service to determine the priority level.
- Investigation: Technical teams analyze the symptoms, gather data, and determine the underlying cause of the service disruption.
- Resolution: Implementing the necessary fix or workaround to restore service to the user.
- Closure: Confirming with the end-user that the service is restored and updating the ticket with comprehensive resolution details before formal closure.

4. ESCALATION MATRIX
When an incident threatens to breach its SLA or requires higher-level authorization, a clear escalation path must be followed:
- Tier 1: Team Lead - The first point of escalation for complex technical hurdles or impending SLA breaches.
- Tier 2: Manager - Engaged when cross-team coordination is required or when client impact becomes significant.
- Tier 3: Director - Escalation point for major service outages requiring substantial resource reallocation.
- Tier 4: Managing Director - Involved only for enterprise-wide crises, severe regulatory implications, or major reputational risks.

5. CUSTOMER FIRST PRINCIPLES
Our operations are guided by a "Customer First" methodology. Regardless of the priority, every raised ticket must receive a formal acknowledgment within 30 minutes of logging. For critical P1 incidents, the service desk is mandated to provide regular, detailed updates to all affected stakeholders every 2 hours until full resolution is achieved.

6. ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS (RCA)
Treating the symptom is not enough; we must cure the disease. For all P1 and recurring P2 incidents, a formal Root Cause Analysis is mandatory. Teams must employ the "5-Why" methodology to drill down to the fundamental process or technical failure. Furthermore, Ishikawa (Fishbone) diagrams should be utilized in the RCA documentation to categorize potential causes (e.g., People, Process, Technology) and ensure a holistic review of the failure points.

7. PERFORMANCE METRICS AND KPIs
Continuous improvement is measured through rigorous tracking of Key Performance Indicators (KPIs). The primary metrics include:
- Mean Time To Resolve (MTTR): The average time taken to fully resolve an incident from the moment it is logged.
- SLA Breach Rate: The percentage of incidents that are not resolved within their designated SLA timeframes.
- First-Contact Resolution (FCR) Rate: The percentage of issues resolved by the service desk on the initial call or contact, reflecting the efficiency of frontline support.
- Operational Efficiency and Error Rate: Tracking the volume of tasks completed against the number of processing errors.
- Processing Accuracy: The paramount metric for financial operations, with a strict organizational target of >99.5% accuracy.

8. DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
Transparency and auditability are non-negotiable. All incidents, service requests, and problem records must be meticulously logged in ServiceNow. This documentation must include a full, unedited audit trail of all actions taken, communications sent, and technical changes applied during the lifecycle of the ticket.

9. CHANGE MANAGEMENT
To protect the production environment, strict governance is applied to all system and process changes. No change can be implemented without formal review and approval from the Change Advisory Board (CAB). In the event of an emergency change required to resolve a P1 incident, standard CAB review may be bypassed, but documented sign-off from a Managing Director is absolutely mandatory before execution.
