DEUTSCHE BANK HR MANAGEMENT POLICIES
DOCUMENT ID: DB-HR-POL-2026-V3.2
CLASSIFICATION: INTERNAL USE ONLY

1. INTRODUCTION TO HR POLICIES
The Deutsche Bank HR Management Policies document outlines the fundamental employment terms, benefits, and workplace expectations for all employees. These policies are designed to foster a supportive, high-performance culture while ensuring compliance with local labor laws and global best practices. This document serves as the primary reference guide for managers and staff regarding human resources administration.

2. ANNUAL LEAVE AND TIME OFF
The bank recognizes the importance of rest and recuperation for sustained high performance. Annual leave entitlements are tiered based on corporate grade:
- Grades 1-5 (Analyst to Assistant Vice President): Entitled to 25 days of paid annual leave per calendar year.
- Grades 6+ (Vice President to Managing Director): Entitled to 30 days of paid annual leave per calendar year.
To encourage employees to take their leave during the year it is accrued, carry-over is strictly limited to a maximum of 5 days into the following calendar year, requiring line manager approval.

3. FLEXIBLE WORKING ARRANGEMENTS
Deutsche Bank supports a flexible working environment to help employees balance professional and personal commitments. 
- Core Hours: The bank operates with mandatory core hours between 11:00 AM and 11:00 PM, during which all staff must be available for collaboration.
- Flexible Start/End: Employees may utilize flexible start times between 7:00 AM and 10:00 AM, and flexible end times between 3:00 PM and 7:00 PM, provided standard weekly hours are met.

4. REMOTE WORKING (HYBRID MODEL)
The bank operates a hybrid working model. Eligible employees may request up to 40% remote working (typically 2 days per week) subject to line manager approval. Approval is contingent upon the requirements of the specific role, sustained performance levels, and the need for in-office team collaboration. Remote working is a privilege, not a contractual right, and can be revoked if operational needs dictate.

5. PERFORMANCE IMPROVEMENT PLAN (PIP)
Deutsche Bank is a high-performance organization. If an employee receives a "Below Expectations" rating during a performance review, or demonstrates a sustained drop in output, they will be placed on a formal Performance Improvement Plan (PIP).
- The PIP is typically a 90-day structured program.
- It must contain clear, measurable milestones and weekly check-ins with the line manager and HR.
- Failure to meet the objectives of the PIP may result in formal disciplinary action.

6. DISCIPLINARY PROCESS
When an employee violates company policy or fails to meet required standards of conduct, a structured disciplinary process is initiated. The standard progression is:
- Step 1: Verbal Warning (documented in HR file).
- Step 2: Written Warning (valid for 6 months).
- Step 3: Final Written Warning (valid for 12 months).
- Step 4: Dismissal.
In cases of gross misconduct (e.g., fraud, severe regulatory breach, harassment), the bank reserves the right to bypass preliminary steps and proceed directly to summary dismissal.

7. GRIEVANCE RESOLUTION PROCESS
The bank provides a formal mechanism for employees to raise concerns regarding their employment, treatment by colleagues, or working conditions.
- Step 1: Employees should first attempt to raise the issue informally with their Line Manager.
- Step 2: If unresolved, a formal grievance is submitted to the assigned HR Business Partner.
- Step 3: If the HRBP resolution is unsatisfactory, it escalates to the Regional HR Director.
- Step 4: In complex cases, external mediation may be offered to reach a resolution.

8. EMPLOYEE BENEFITS PACKAGE
Deutsche Bank offers a comprehensive, competitive benefits package designed to support employee health and financial security. Key benefits include:
- Private Medical Insurance: Comprehensive health coverage for the employee, with options to add dependents.
- Pension Scheme: A generous non-contributory pension plan with an 8% employer contribution based on base salary.
- Life Assurance: Coverage equal to 4× the employee's annual base salary paid to designated beneficiaries in the event of death in service.

9. WELLBEING AND MENTAL HEALTH
The wellbeing of our workforce is a strategic priority. The bank provides an Employee Assistance Programme (EAP) offering free, confidential counseling and support services 24/7. Furthermore, the bank maintains a network of certified Mental Health First Aiders embedded within every major team to provide immediate peer support and signposting to professional resources.

10. DIVERSITY & INCLUSION (D&I)
We are committed to an inclusive culture where everyone can thrive. Mandatory D&I training is required for all employees. Deutsche Bank operates a strict zero-tolerance policy regarding any form of discrimination, bullying, or harassment based on race, gender, age, sexual orientation, disability, or religion.

11. THE EXIT PROCESS
When an employee leaves the bank, standard notice periods apply to ensure a smooth transition:
- Grades 1-4: 1-month mandatory notice period.
- Grades 5+: 3-months mandatory notice period.
Before departure, an exit interview with HR is mandatory to gather feedback on the employee experience and recover all corporate IT assets.
